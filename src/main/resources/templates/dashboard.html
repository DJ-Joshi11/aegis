How AEGIS works

Four stages, each running as its own package in one Spring Boot app, talking to each other over real events and REST — not four slides. Click a stage to see what it does and why it exists.

STAGE 1 Decision Ingestion Audited so far: 0
What's happening
Qusar-style agent decisions — underwriting calls, claims outcomes, fraud flags — stream in continuously from agents across 7 jurisdictions.
Why
You can't govern a decision that was never recorded. Every downstream stage depends on a trustworthy audit log existing first.
What happens
Each decision is validated, normalized, and persisted as an immutable audit record, then broadcast in-process to detection.
Problem it solves
Agentic AI in insurance today can run with no consistent, queryable audit trail — a claim gets approved or denied by an agent and nobody outside that agent's own logs knows it happened.
→
STAGE 2 Detection & Compliance Flagged so far: 0
What's happening
Every decision is checked in real time against two rules: an always-flag fraud signal, and a statistical denial-rate outlier per agent.
Why
Regulators — the EU AI Act, UK FCA, IRDAI, and similar bodies elsewhere — increasingly require human-reviewable explainability for automated decisions.
What happens
A flagged decision is logged with a plain-English reason and automatically routed to the correct jurisdiction's compliance standard.
Problem it solves
Manual compliance review can't keep pace with agent decision volume. This closes that gap without slowing the agent down.
→
STAGE 3 Payout Engine Triggered so far: 0
What's happening
Flagged decisions and unresolved compliance reviews (an SLA breach — past 45 seconds with no clearance) both trigger an automatic payout.
Why
AEGIS isn't only a governance layer, it's a coverage product: if the carrier's own AI agent makes a bad call, or a flagged case isn't reviewed in time, someone has to be made whole automatically.
What happens
A payout record is created and logged instantly, with the trigger reason attached — no manual claims-adjuster step for these cases.
Problem it solves
Today, an AI misfire either gets no compensation at all, or requires a slow manual claims process to correct.
→
STAGE 4 Pricing Feedback Agents priced: 0
What's happening
Every agent's error rate — payouts triggered divided by decisions made — feeds a live premium multiplier for that specific agent.
Why
Insurance pricing has to reflect real risk. An AI agent with a high error rate is a higher-risk asset to insure, exactly like a human underwriter with a bad track record would be.
What happens
The multiplier recalculates continuously and is exposed per-agent over its own API, ready to plug into a real underwriting engine.
Problem it solves
There's currently no mechanism tying an AI agent's actual track record back into what it costs to insure that agent's decisions going forward.

Why this matters for Guidewire

Where it sits

Qusar is the agentic layer making autonomous calls across PolicyCenter-style underwriting and ClaimCenter-style claims workflows. AEGIS sits alongside it as an independent governance-and-insurance layer — it doesn't replace Qusar's decisioning, it watches, checks, and financially backstops it.

What's genuinely new here

Most AI governance tooling insures the policyholder. AEGIS is built to insure the AI agent's own decisions — a product for carriers who are themselves the ones deploying agentic AI and need to cover the risk that creates.

Compliance that maps to real regulation

Flagged decisions aren't just logged — they're routed to the specific explainability standard that applies to that decision's jurisdiction (EU AI Act Art.14, UK FCA governance principles, IRDAI's algorithmic disclosure rules, and more), so review is targeted, not generic.

A built-in economic feedback loop

Static governance rules go stale. AEGIS's pricing engine means an agent's real error rate continuously changes what it costs to insure that agent — the incentive to keep decisions clean is economic, not just procedural.

Honest caveat: we don't have access to a live Qusar sandbox for this build. MockDecisionGenerator emits decisions in the exact shape a real Qusar agent's decision log would take (agent ID, decision type, outcome, jurisdiction, reasoning trail) so the entire downstream pipeline — detection, compliance mapping, payout, pricing — is built and tested against that real shape. Connecting a live Qusar feed would mean swapping out this one mock generator; nothing downstream of ingestion would need to change.
